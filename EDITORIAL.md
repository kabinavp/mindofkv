# Editorial standard

Set by KV, 13 Nov 2026. **Non-negotiable.** Every piece published under Mind of KV (model essays, opinions,
Low Gear issues, LinkedIn posts, Substack Notes) meets this standard. Each new piece should raise the bar.
Never publish one that's worse than the best before it.

**Roles.** Claude is editor-in-chief: researches, drafts, edits, fact-checks and holds the bar.
KV is the author: the ideas and experiences are his, and nothing goes out without his approval (via the Low Gear Desk).

## Benchmarks

- **Farnam Street (fs.blog):** research depth, correct attribution to original thinkers, thoughtful essays,
  dense cross-linking between models.
- **Lenny's Newsletter:** practical, product-grounded writing, with frameworks, real examples and templates
  readers can use straight away.

## The six tests every piece must pass

1. **A new learning.** Before drafting, write the takeaway in one sentence. If a thoughtful reader would already know it,
   find a sharper angle or don't publish. Every reader should leave with something they didn't have.
2. **Researched and attributed.** Credit every borrowed idea to its originator, using the primary source:
   person, work, year and a link. Check quotes against a primary or reputable source. If a quote can't be verified,
   cut it or label it "attributed to". Use no statistic without a source. Model essays end with a **Sources** list.
3. **KV's experience is the proof.** Each piece rests on a real decision, user or team from KV's own work, in his
   own words. Never invent experiences, opinions or numbers (see CLAUDE.md).
4. **Thought leadership, not summary.** Add something the sources don't have: a distinction KV draws, when the
   model fails or misleads, a counter-argument taken seriously, or a link between two ideas.
5. **Cross-referenced and coherent.** Link each piece to at least two related models, and make sure they link back.
   Use the same names for the same ideas everywhere. A reader should be able to wander the site like a library.
6. **Clear.** Use plain words and short sentences, and make every sentence earn its place. No jargon in headlines.
   It should read like a person thinking carefully, not a content machine.

## Shape of a model essay

1. **Hook:** a real situation where the question mattered.
2. **The model in one sentence,** within the first 100 words.
3. **Origin and evidence:** who first described it, and what research supports it, properly credited.
4. **How it works:** steps or questions, concrete enough to use.
5. **KV's example:** the decision, what happened, what he learned.
6. **Where it breaks:** limits, failure modes, when to use something else.
7. **Use it on Monday:** the template and a first step.
8. **Related models:** cross-links, each with a sentence on how it relates.
9. **Sources.**

## Formats

| Format | Length | Must have |
|---|---|---|
| Model essay | 1,200–2,500 words | All of the shape above; template; sources |
| Opinion | 1,000–2,500 words | A clear position, the strongest counter-argument, sources |
| Low Gear issue | 600 words max (5 minutes) | One idea with its source credited, a Monday action, a question, KV's book line, a verified quote |
| LinkedIn post | 1,300 characters max | Hook in the first two lines (LinkedIn cuts off at about 210 characters); one real example; the originator credited; share image from `/share/<id>.png`; link in the first comment |
| Substack Note | 1–3 sentences | One sharp idea, credited |

## SEO (every page)

- **Title:** model name plus the benefit, under 60 characters. **Slug:** the model name.
- **Meta description:** 140–160 characters, with the promise and the main keyword.
- One H1. H2s that read as an outline of the piece and use the words people search for.
- Answer the main question in the first paragraph.
- Internal links with descriptive anchor text (never "click here"). Alt text on every image.
- Structured data and a share image (already built into the site).

## Before anything reaches the desk

- [ ] Takeaway in one sentence; it's new to a thoughtful reader
- [ ] Every claim, quote and number checked against a source; sources listed
- [ ] KV's experience in his own words; nothing invented
- [ ] Adds an insight the sources don't have
- [ ] At least 2 cross-links in, at least 2 out; consistent model names
- [ ] SEO fields done (title, description, H2s, alt text)
- [ ] Within the length for its format
- [ ] Better than the best piece before it. If not, keep working.
