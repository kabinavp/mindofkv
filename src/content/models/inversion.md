Most planning asks “how do we make this win?” Inversion flips the question: **how could this flop?** It borrows from Charlie Munger, who said all he wanted to know was where he was going to die, so he’d never go there.

Working backwards from failure exposes blind spots faster than chasing wins. Users rarely tell you what’s broken; inverting helps you find it first. It’s also a humble way to think. You start by admitting you’re fallible.

## Questions to ask

- How could this launch flop?
- What will make people leave after the first use?
- Why might the customers we expect ignore us entirely?
- What assumption, if wrong, sinks the whole thing?

## When to reach for it

Before a launch, when a plan looks too clean, or when a team is only discussing upside.
