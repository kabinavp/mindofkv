OKRs promise direction and measurable outcomes. For small teams they often bring complexity instead, taking time away from the one advantage a small team has: speed.

The alternative is a light rhythm. **Plan on Monday, execute through the week, review on Friday.** Set weekly deliverables, and keep people accountable without a rigid goal structure.

When execution slows, the cause is rarely a missing OKR. More often the team is spending its time defining key results instead of delivering them.

## It doesn’t mean no structure

It means choosing the smallest structure that keeps everyone honest, and adding more only when the team is big enough to need it.
