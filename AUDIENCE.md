# Who Mind of KV is for, and how we reach them

Draft for KV to confirm. Points marked **[confirm]** are assumptions, not facts from KV.

## The reader in one line

**The newly accountable:** people who recently became responsible for what gets built, but don't have the
authority to just decide. They have to persuade, trade off and commit, often for the first time.

## Who they are

| | Core (the person we write for) | Also served (same problems, different title) |
|---|---|---|
| Roles | Product people, about 3–12 years in: first-time owners of a product area, new team leads | Founders of small teams; engineers becoming tech leads or EMs; designers leading a product area |
| Where | Indian tech: Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Pune, Hyderabad, plus Indians working abroad **[confirm]** | Anyone in a building role, anywhere |
| Situation | Promoted for doing the work well; now judged on decisions and on other people's work | Same |

## What they struggle with (the jobs Low Gear does)

1. **Making a hard call under uncertainty.** *Deciding:* Inversion, Pre-mortem.
2. **Moving people they don't manage.** *Working with people:* 3 I's of Influence, Default High Trust.
3. **Knowing whether they are getting better.** *Getting better:* Honest Gap Analysis, Momentum over Measurement.
4. **Making sense of the wider world they build in.** *Seeing the world:* Opinions on education, taxation and more.

They already have plenty of frameworks. What they lack is **a tool they can use this week, shown working on a real decision.**

## The promise

> One way to think through a hard call, every Sunday. Five minutes.

Every issue and every model page has to deliver on all three parts:

- **One:** a single idea, never a roundup.
- **Hard call:** shown on a real decision, with a user or a team in it.
- **Five minutes:** no more than 600 words. If it's longer, cut it.

**Sharpness test before anything goes out:** can the reader say what they'll do differently on Monday?
If not, it isn't ready.

## How we reach them

1. **LinkedIn, 2–3 posts a week.** Each post follows the site's rule: a universal principle as the hook, a real
   product story as the proof, and the model card PNG as the image. Link to the model page in the first comment.
   About 3 posts per model.
2. **Comment before posting.** Spend 15 minutes a day leaving thoughtful comments on bigger writers' posts aimed at the same readers.
   Early on, comments bring more reach than posts.
3. **Templates as the reason to sign up.** Every model page offers its template. Later, the
   "3 I's of Influence" self-assessment adds subscribers to Kit with a tag.
4. **Substack Recommendations.** Swap recommendations with 3–5 writers of a similar size who write for
   builders but aren't competitors.
5. **One talk or guest spot a quarter.** A ProductTank meetup, a podcast or a community AMA, each built around one model.

## What to measure

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| **Replies to "One question"** | The best sign the issue landed. Aim for a few every week. |
| Forwards and new signups from LinkedIn | Shows whether the reach engine is working |
| Open rate | Health check only; don't chase it |
| Subscriber count | Kept private until it's real (`SITE.newsletter.subscribers` stays null until then) |

## What we don't do

- No roundups of links or "10 frameworks" lists.
- No PM jargon in headlines (see CLAUDE.md).
- No made-up numbers, testimonials or subscriber counts.
- No engagement bait on LinkedIn ("Agree?", polls with no point).
